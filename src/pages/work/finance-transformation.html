The challenge

A leading European bank had grown through mergers and acquisitions, leaving it running multiple institutions, each with its own systems and accounting frameworks. Maintaining statutory and regulatory compliance across that landscape relied on complex, costly workarounds, while regulators demanded ever higher granularity, quality, consistency and frequency of data (BCBS 239) under sustained pressure on operating costs.

The approach

As a subject-matter expert, I helped shape an application to enable finance transformation, centralising and aligning data and accounting streams into a single general-ledger capability. It captures accounted, contract, counterparty and reference data as one source of truth, reuses existing subledgers with event-based accounting to fill gaps, and adds automated reconciliation for balance sheet, P&L and financial disclosures. A control framework with validations, exception and suspense handling, automated journals and dashboards keeps data quality high with minimal manual intervention.

The outcome

The bank gained high-quality, granular, daily accounting data and a single source of truth for financial figures, with automated reconciliation and a transparent control framework. Decommissioning legacy systems simplified the application landscape, reduced manual effort and lowered the cost of the Finance function, while supporting regulatory demands such as BCBS 239 and SRB reporting.
